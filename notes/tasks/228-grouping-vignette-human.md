## Summary

The vignette is built on the Lum-31 bioluminescent bacterial assay of Luter
et al. (2025), with `herbicide` used for the factor-covariate section. The
syntax section is written and merged into the branch. What remains is the
dataset, the case studies, and the argument that ties them together.

That argument is new, and it is why this dataset was chosen. Dividing a response
by its control mean is standard practice in ecotoxicology and Ritz et al. (2026)
show it biases effect concentrations downwards and makes confidence intervals
too narrow. Their recommended remedy is to model the raw response and treat the
control level as a parameter. A group-level term on the control parameter does
exactly that, one curve per plate, with the uncertainty propagated. The grouping
machinery is therefore the answer to a problem users already have, rather than a
facility they may or may not need.

---

## The dataset

`lum31` is the acute copper and zinc data from Luter et al. (2025), a
bioluminescent bacterial toxicity assay developed at AIMS. The response is raw
luminescence in relative light units.

The design supplies all three structures the vignette teaches, which no other
dataset examined does:

| structure | what it is | term |
|---|---|---|
| well | four replicate wells per concentration, per plate | `ogl()` |
| plate | 16 copper and 17 zinc plates, each spanning the whole series | `pgl()`, `(par \| plate)` |
| toxicant and exposure time | copper against zinc, 15 minutes against 30 | `bnec_group()` |

The data are published, the analysis code is public at
`open-AIMS/Lum31-tox-assays`, and RF is an author. The source workbooks are held
in `data-raw/lum31/` and are not tracked.

Two properties of the response need handling and are covered in their own
sections.

**The zeros are floored, not observed.** Readings were blank-corrected, so a
negative result was replaced by zero. Luminescence cannot be negative, so these
are noise about a small positive value rather than a distinct state. They are
left-censored rather than modelled with a hurdle. `Gamma` refuses an exact zero,
so the censoring bound is the smallest resolved reading rather than zero itself.

**The plate gain is set automatically.** The plate reader applies auto-scale gain
adjustment, so absolute luminescence is not comparable between plates. Gain is a
per-plate multiplicative constant, which is the exact form a group-level term
takes on a positive response. Modelling it is therefore an alternative to
dividing it out, and the better one.

## Vignette structure

Seven sections, in this order. The first is written and merged.

**1. Group-level term syntax.** *Already on the branch.* States what `brms`
formula each of `ogl()`, `pgl()` and `(par | group)` produces, generated by
`make_brmsformula()` so it cannot drift from what the package fits, with a
`gaussian()` contrast and a mapping from design structure to term.

**2. Normalising to a control.** *Purpose:* to give the reader a reason to use a
group-level term that does not depend on them already wanting one. States the
Ritz et al. (2026) result, shows that the Lum-31 assay's published analysis
divides each plate by four control wells, and shows that a group-level term on
the control parameter targets the same quantity while propagating its
uncertainty. *Done when:* a reader can state why the raw response is modelled.

**3. Preparing the response.** *Purpose:* the censoring and the family have to be
settled before any fit, and both are consequences of how the instrument works.
Covers blank correction, the floored zeros, the censoring bound and the choice of
`Gamma`. *Done when:* the prepared dataset is reproducible from one chunk.

**4. Within-concentration grouping.** *Purpose:* the commonest design in
ecotoxicology, and the one where only a displacement is estimable. Fits
`ogl(well)` against an ungrouped fit on the same data. *Done when:* both fits
converge and the section states what the term changed.

**5. Across-concentration grouping.** *Purpose:* the case the package could not
fit before #295, and the one the normalisation argument rests on. Fits
`(top | plate)` and `pgl(plate)`, and compares the estimated plate factor with
the ratio the published analysis divides by. *Done when:* the comparison is
tabulated.

**6. Design and model complexity.** *Purpose:* a design with few concentrations
cannot support a curve with many parameters, and the failure is a sampler
failure rather than an error. Uses `CoralColour` and `pamdat` from Flores et al.
(2021): `pamdat` with `ogl(chamber)` is a clean 54-chamber within-concentration
fit, and `CoralColour` has five concentrations, where equations of four
parameters give 291 divergent transitions of 800 and equations of three give
none. *Done when:* the contrast is tabulated and #301 is referenced.

**7. Factor covariates.** *Purpose:* levels that are of interest in themselves
are not a random grouping, and the distinction is the one users get wrong.
`bnec_group()` on `herbicide`, which reproduces the final figure of the JSS paper
in a single call, and on `lum31` by toxicant. *Done when:* the seven herbicide
levels are fitted and compared with `compare_posterior()`.

## Effect on the package interface

Nothing in the package changes. The vignette documents behaviour that already
exists, and the one addition it argues for is recorded separately as #301, a way
to restrict a model set by the number of curve parameters.

## Questions for Heidi Luter

Both concern the censoring bound and neither blocks the plan.

1. Do the per-plate blank well readings still exist? The blank spread would give
   a principled detection limit. Without it the bound is the smallest resolved
   reading, which is defensible but arbitrary.
2. Was the plate reader gain recorded alongside the luminescence? If it was,
   absolute luminescence becomes comparable between plates and the plate-level
   variance could be separated into instrumental and biological parts. If it was
   not, that variance is a nuisance term and cannot be interpreted biologically.

## Rejected datasets

**`coral`** (Brinkman et al. 2023). 73 per cent of observations on a boundary, no
arm converges, and redistribution unconfirmed. Not on the branch.

**`nassarius`.** Growth is a `hurdle_gamma` endpoint, so the existing examples no
longer hold. Survival is a step function with no tank between 0 and 1 for
contaminant C.

**`example_dat.csv`**, the Antarctic set. The strongest structure after `lum31`,
and both grouping terms fit it, but its origin cannot be established. A manual
page would have to state a source that does not exist.

**`example_pgl.csv`**, the 2023 Lum-31 extract. Scaled to the control and then to
the plate maximum, then multiplied by 0.999 to clear the `Beta` boundary. The raw
workbooks supersede it.

**`sea_urchin`**, the four copper reference tests in `toxtools`. The clearest
demonstration found that a group-level term rescues a fit that pooling breaks,
but only the first of the four tests is published and the source workbook is not
redistributed. Kept for the training material if that is ever settled.

Provenance and redistribution for every dataset screened are recorded in
`notes/dataset_provenance.md`.
